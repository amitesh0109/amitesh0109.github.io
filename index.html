    How to read this chart

    Imagine standing in Hyderabad holding a compass. Each line points the way you'd set off to reach that country by the shortest route, and its length is how far that is. Kenya, for example, is about 5,000 km away, heading west-southwest.

    Why are the lines straight? The Earth is a ball, so the shortest route between two cities looks curved on a flat world map. This chart is drawn from Hyderabad's point of view, which turns every shortest route from home into a straight line.

    The maths, in one sentence: treat the Earth as a ball 6,371 km in radius, use school trigonometry to find the angle between two cities as seen from the Earth's centre, and multiply that angle by the radius to get the distance.

    See the formulas

    For cities at latitude φ and longitude λ:

    σ = arccos( sin φ₁ sin φ₂ + cos φ₁ cos φ₂ cos Δλ )
    distance = 6,371 km × σ
    direction θ = atan2( sin Δλ cos φ₂ , cos φ₁ sin φ₂ − sin φ₁ cos φ₂ cos Δλ )

    Each point is then drawn at that direction, at a distance from the centre scaled to the 8,000 km outer ring. Mapmakers call this an azimuthal equidistant projection.
